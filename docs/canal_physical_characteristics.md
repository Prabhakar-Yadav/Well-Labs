## 1. Indian Irrigation Canal Hierarchy — Standard Classification (per IIT Kharagpur / BIS)

| Canal Level | Discharge (cumecs = m3/s) | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Main Canal** | Largest; carries full river diversion | Feeder only — no direct irrigation |
| **Branch Canal** | > 5 m3/s | Feeds major distributaries |
| **Major Distributary** | 0.25 – 5 m3/s | Feeds watercourses or minor distributaries |
| **Minor Distributary** | < 0.25 m3/s | Feeds watercourses / field channels |
| **Watercourse / Field Channel (FIC)** | < 0.06 m3/s (< ~2 cusecs) | Delivers water to individual fields |

**Key threshold**: The 0.25 m3/s boundary separates "major" from "minor" distributaries. A single outlet (chak) typically runs at 0.03 m3/s (30 lit/sec = 1 cusec).

## 3. Estimated Physical Dimensions by Canal Level

These are derived from Indian engineering standards (IS 10430, IS 7112), Lacey's regime equations, NPTEL lessons, and typical south Indian practice. **No single public document gives exact TLBC cross-sections**, but the following are well-grounded estimates:

### Main Canal (TLBC Main)
- **Discharge**: ~116 m3/s at head
- **Bed width**: 20–30 m (the NPTEL design example shows 30m bed width for a canal in the ~100 m3/s range)
- **Full supply depth (FSD)**: 2.5–4.0 m
- **Top width (at water surface)**: 30–45 m (depending on side slopes)
- **Side slopes**: 1.5H:1V to 2H:1V
- **Lining**: Concrete-lined (CC lining, 5–12 cm thick per BIS)
- **Freeboard**: 0.75 m (per IS 10430 for Q > 10 m3/s)
- **Bank top width**: 7–8 m (inspection road side), 3.5–5 m (non-inspection side)
- **Appearance from above**: Wide, straight/gently curving, concrete-gray or light-colored bed, distinct raised banks, inspection road visible alongside

### Branch Canal
- **Discharge**: 5–30 m3/s (could be higher near main canal)
- **Bed width**: 8–20 m
- **FSD**: 1.5–2.5 m
- **Top width**: 12–30 m
- **Lining**: Typically concrete-lined
- **Freeboard**: 0.6–0.75 m
- **Bank top width**: 5–6 m (inspection), 2.5 m (non-inspection)
- **Appearance from above**: Similar to main canal but narrower; concrete-lined; clear bank structure

### Major Distributary (0.25–5 m3/s)
- **Bed width**: 1.5–8 m
- **FSD**: 0.8–1.8 m
- **Top width**: 3–12 m
- **Side slopes**: 1.5H:1V (lined), 2H:1V (unlined)
- **Lining**: Often concrete-lined at the larger end (1–5 m3/s); may be brick-tile or unlined at the smaller end
- **Freeboard**: 0.50 m (for 1–5 m3/s), 0.30 m (for < 1 m3/s)
- **Bank top width**: 5 m (inspection), 1.5–2.5 m (non-inspection)
- **Appearance from above**: Clearly defined trapezoidal channel, narrower than branch canal, may show concrete or earth-colored bed

### Minor Distributary (< 0.25 m3/s)
- **Discharge**: 0.06–0.25 m3/s (~2–9 cusecs)
- **Bed width**: 0.5–1.5 m
- **FSD**: 0.4–0.8 m
- **Top width**: 1.5–4 m
- **Lining**: Often unlined (earthen); may have brick/tile or shotcrete lining in modernized sections
- **Freeboard**: 0.30 m
- **Appearance from above**: Narrow channel, often earthen-brown, vegetation on banks common, less regular than larger canals

### Field Irrigation Channel / Watercourse (FIC) (< 0.06 m3/s)
- **Discharge**: 0.01–0.06 m3/s (roughly 0.3–2 cusecs); single outlet = 0.03 m3/s (1 cusec)
- **Bed width**: 0.15–0.5 m (6 inches to 1.5 feet)
- **FSD**: 0.15–0.4 m
- **Top width**: 0.3–1.0 m
- **Side slopes**: variable; often irregular, especially if earthen
- **Lining**: Predominantly **earthen/unlined**. Under modernization programs (e.g., AIBP, Jal Jeevan Mission, ADB-funded Karnataka projects), some FICs receive concrete or brick lining, but the vast majority remain earthen
- **Freeboard**: 0.1–0.15 m (per IS 10430)
- **Appearance from above**: Very narrow, irregular, brown/tan colored, often with vegetation, connects to a distributary at one end and terminates at field plots

## 4. Canal Lining in the TLBC System

### General Indian Practice (per NPTEL / BIS)
- **Concrete lining** (CC): Best suited for main canals carrying large quantities at high velocity. Thickness 5–12 cm. Side slopes 1.25H:1V to 1.5H:1V. Max velocity ~2.7 m/s.
- **Brick/tile lining**: Popular for medium channels (distributaries). No rigid quality control needed. Velocity limit ~1.8 m/s.
- **Boulder lining**: For rocky areas. Velocity limit ~1.5 m/s.
- **Earth lining types**: Stabilized earth, loose earth blankets, compacted earth, soil-cement.
- **Unlined**: Cheapest but suffers seepage, vegetation growth, erosion. Manning's n ~0.02 for small canals (< 15 m3/s), vs ~0.015-0.018 for concrete.

### TLBC-Specific Lining (best available information)
| Canal Level | Typical Lining Status |
|---|---|
| Main Canal | **Concrete-lined** (CC lining). Under modernization, being upgraded for increased capacity. |
| Branch Canals | **Mostly concrete-lined** |
| Major Distributaries | **Mixed** — larger ones concrete-lined, smaller ones may be brick-tile or unlined |
| Minor Distributaries | **Mostly unlined (earthen)** — some being lined under modernization |
| FICs / Watercourses | **Predominantly earthen/unlined** — some pilot lining under ADB-funded programs |

The Government of Karnataka, under ADB Tranche-2 and AIBP programs, has been modernizing the TLBC system with CC lining of selected distributaries. Distributary D-95 (your study area near Raichur/Atnur) was selected as a pilot site for modernization under the Karnataka Land & Water Management initiative.

## 5. Field Bunds vs FICs — Distinguishing in Drone Imagery

### Physical Differences
| Feature | Field Bund | FIC (Field Irrigation Channel) |
|---|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Retains water within a paddy plot | Conveys water from distributary to plots |
| **Shape** | Closed loops / polygons surrounding fields | Open-ended linear feature connecting to a distributary |
| **Width** | 0.1–0.3 m (narrow ridges) | 0.15–0.5 m (narrow channels with banks) |
| **Height/Depth** | Raised 0.1–0.3 m above field level | Depressed 0.15–0.4 m below field level |
| **Material** | Earthen (compacted mud) | Earthen (sometimes lined) |
| **Connectivity** | Connected only to adjacent bunds; forms closed perimeters | Connected at one end to a distributary canal |
| **Water presence** | Water on TOP of bund (inside the paddy) | Water INSIDE the channel |
| **Vegetation** | Often grassy on top | Often has water/mud/vegetation inside |

### Visual Differences in Drone Imagery (4.6 cm/px resolution)
1. **Topology**: FICs are **tree-like** (branching from distributary). Bunds are **grid/polygon** patterns.
2. **Connectivity**: FICs always connect to a larger canal at one end. Bunds form closed rectangular shapes with no canal connection.
3. **Width**: FICs are typically slightly wider (0.15–0.5 m = 3–11 px) than bunds (0.1–0.3 m = 2–7 px), but there is significant overlap.
4. **Spectral signature**: Nearly identical (both tan/brown earthen). **Cannot be separated by color alone.**
5. **Depth cues**: FICs cast shadows (being depressed channels) while bunds cast shadows on the opposite side (being raised). At solar noon this difference may be minimal.
6. **Water/moisture**: During irrigation season, FICs may show water or wet-soil reflectance inside. Bunds show water on both sides (paddy fields) but the bund itself is dry on top.

### Recommended Detection Strategy
- Use **width** as primary separator from larger canals
- Use **connectivity analysis** (graph-based) to distinguish FICs from bunds:
  - FIC = narrow linear feature with at least one endpoint touching a distributary
  - Bund = narrow linear feature forming closed loops, no connection to canal network
- This matches the strategy already noted in project_canal.md

## 8. Key Caveats

1. **No public TLBC engineering drawings found**: The exact bed widths and cross-sections for the TLBC main canal and its specific distributaries are not published in publicly accessible documents. The numbers above are derived from Indian engineering standards and regime equations applied to the known discharge.

2. **Modernization changes**: The TLBC has been undergoing modernization since ~2010 (ADB funding, AIBP). Some previously unlined distributaries may now be lined. D-95 specifically was a pilot modernization site.

3. **Actual vs design**: Many canals in the TLBC system are decades old and have experienced silting, vegetation encroachment, and bank erosion. The actual visible cross-section may differ significantly from design dimensions.

4. **Seasonal variation**: During kharif season (rice), FICs and bunds are both submerged/saturated. During rabi or dry season, channels may be dry and harder to distinguish from bunds.
